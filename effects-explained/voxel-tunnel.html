1. Two maps describe the surface

This is a height-field voxel effect, not a solid cube of 3D cells. For each of the 256 × 256 map locations, one byte says how high the surface is; a second byte chooses its color. The executable loads both maps from TZ1300.DAT. It builds the visible voxel image from them every frame.

Why a sample becomes a vertical span

For each column, the routine walks from near to far through depth samples 0–96. A lookup table converts height and depth into a vertical coordinate:

source = column × 256 + ((counter + depth) & 255)
height = heightMap[source]
color  = texture[source]

projectedY = max(0,
  trunc(height × 32 / (depth + 1)) − 10)

The write cursor begins at row 199. If a new sample projects above it, the renderer fills the gap upwards with that sample's color. Otherwise the sample is hidden and writes nothing. The cursor never moves back down: a later sample cannot overwrite nearer visible spans.

3. Turn the strip into a tunnel

The voxel buffer is not perspective-warped with triangles. Every screen pixel looks up its distance and angle from the center. Distance selects a row of the voxel image; angle selects a column. Straight columns become radial spokes, and horizontal rows become rings.

Point at or touch the tunnel to trace a pixel back into the voxel buffer. A small pink square on the voxel buffer marks its exact source byte while you inspect the tunnel. Diagnostic views show lookup coordinates, not production colors.

One screen pixel, one buffer byte

dx = x − 160; dy = y − 100
radius = trunc(sqrt(dx² + dy²))
angle  = trunc(atan2(dy, dx) × 256 / (2π)) & 255

rotation = sineTable[(counter × 8) & 4095] >> 9
u = (angle + rotation) & 255
v = radius
screen[y × 320 + x] = buffer[v × 256 + u]

The demo precomputes angle and radius tables during setup. The live mapping here reads those same tables. The sine table gives a small changing angular offset; this rotates the pattern around the tunnel.

A useful experiment: complete the voxel buffer, select Angle lookup, then move the counter. Switch back to the original colors to see how angular movement carries the texture around the center. Next, clear the mapping and reveal the screen one row at a time.

There is no extra tunnel texture

The tunnel's texture is the newly constructed voxel buffer itself. Its dark inner boundary is the unfilled part of the height-field projection. Repeating ridges turn into the surrounding wall when read with polar coordinates.

4. Add the red spiky object and green credits

The spiky object is rendered separately by the original polygon engine. The green credits use additional palette entries and image layers. They are not generated by the voxel columns.
